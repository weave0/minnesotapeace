- **Sanction discrepancy RESOLVED (2026-08-30 pass).** Retrieved the actual June 24, 2021 order text (Index #95, `src-mncro-62-cv-20-5492-order-2021-06-24-contempt`) directly from MCRO. It was never a real discrepancy: the court ordered a **$35,750** contempt sanction *plus* a separately-stipulated **$11,750** in attorneys' fees, totaling **$47,500** -- which is exactly what Sahan Journal's "more than $47,000" reported. - **April 30 materials now partly retrieved.** FOF's April 30 reply brief (`src-mncro-62-cv-20-5492-reply-2021-04-30`) and Second Amended Complaint (`src-mncro-62-cv-20-5492-second-amended-complaint-2021-04-30`) are in the corpus. ## 13. April 2021 MDE payment-resumption decision record

- **Substantially resolved as to event, date, decision path, and remembered rationale.** OLA records that MDE removed the Feeding Our Future stop-pay on **2021-04-30**, nine days after the April 21 Ramsey County hearing, and says MDE did so because of the court's April 21 statements. The Minnesota Judicial Branch independently confirms the court never ordered payment resumption and that MDE resumed voluntarily.
- A newly captured primary congressional transcribed interview with then-MDE Assistant Commissioner **Daron Korte** (`src-house-oversight-2026-02-03-daron-korte-interview`) supplies the participant-level chain that was previously missing. Korte recalled the internal decision included himself, **Monica Herrera**, and **Emily Honer**; he said then-Commissioner **Heather Mueller** was informed how MDE would proceed rather than asked for approval; and he said the Attorney General attorneys representing MDE in the lawsuit were involved.
- Korte said he did **not** think the Governor's Office participated in making the decision, although he would have updated its education policy and legal advisers. Preserve that distinction: notification is not decision-making or direction.
- Korte's stated rationale was that MDE believed forcing a judicial ruling was highly likely to produce the same substantive outcome plus additional contempt/attorney-fee sanctions. He also recalled that MDE expected or knew of federal law-enforcement involvement and hoped resumed payments would be short-lived until the FBI/U.S. Attorney's Office intervened. He said the FBI never instructed MDE to resume payments.
- Honer's separate transcribed interview corroborates the March 31 stop-pay and lack of a final court ruling, but she did **not** remember the post-hearing deliberations about whether the stop-pay would continue. That memory limit should not be converted into either corroboration or contradiction of Korte's account.
- **Still open:** no contemporaneous internal MDE email chain, meeting notes, memorandum, or stipulation memorializing the April 30 decision has been captured. Korte said he did not believe he signed such a document. The exact substance of any privileged advice from the Attorney General's litigating attorneys also remains unavailable.
- **Sequencing question RESOLVED (2026-08-30 pass).** FOF's reply brief, filed before the April 30 hearing, quotes MDE's written opposition as saying the stop-pay was removed "pursuant to the Court's guidance at the April 21, 2021, hearing." The official April 30 hearing transcript now independently records MDE counsel stating that MDE was not continuing the stop-pay and that the stop-pay had been removed. MDE counsel further said the claims were expected to appear in CLiCS after 5:00 p.m. that day as scheduled to be paid the following Thursday or Friday. This closes the causal/sequencing question while sharpening the remaining payment-timing issue: **April 30 is the administrative stop-pay-removal/return-to-processing date, not necessarily the date every reimbursement dollar cleared or was received.**
- The June 24, 2021 written order independently confirms that MDE had lifted the stop-pay and paid the reimbursement claims by the time of that order. The standalone MDE April 30 written opposition remains worth retrieving for exact written phrasing and any arguments not aired orally, but it is no longer a factual blocker to the core chronology.
- **Daron Korte context:** FOF's Second Amended Complaint, filed the evening of 2021-04-30, named Korte personally ("officially and personally") as a defendant alongside MDE. He was therefore a personal litigant in this matter when he later gave his 2026 congressional account. That fact belongs in the record but does not establish inaccuracy; his specific recollections checked here are substantially corroborated by contemporaneous records.
- **149 vs. 143 RESOLVED AS DIFFERENT COHORTS/STAGES.** The April 29 MDE action included **149 site-ID denials**; the June 24 contempt order addressed **144 applications submitted during the court-defined Nov. 30–Apr. 13 cohort**, one of which was approved and **143** denied. OLA independently confirms both the April 29 149 figure and that MDE later agreed to process **143 of those 149**. The remaining research gap is not which total is “right,” but the exact mapping of the six additional April 29 site-ID denials outside the order's 144-application cohort.

One claim per heading. Compiled 2026-08-26 (America/Chicago). Actor classes: (A) private actors defrauded programs; (B) agency failed to detect/prevent; (C) negligence/incompetence; (D) officials knowingly committed crimes. Do not collapse B/C into D.

- **evidence_class:** AUDIT_FINDING
- **status:** Verified, with MDE written dispute.
- **event_date:** report released 2024-06-13
- **source urls:**
  - https://www.auditor.leg.state.mn.us/sreview/pdf/2024-mdefof.pdf (S26)
  - https://www.auditor.leg.state.mn.us/sreview/2024/mdefof.htm
  - https://www.auditor.leg.state.mn.us/sreview/2024/mdefof-sum.htm (S27)
- **amounts + metric_type:** OLA transmittal recites federal charging of “70 individuals” and a “$250 million fraud scheme” as the federal government’s allegation — **alleged_loss** / **fraud_estimate**, attributed to DOJ, not independently audited as a loss total by OLA.
- **is_allegation:** OLA findings are audit conclusions about oversight (B/C), not criminal charges against MDE (not D).
- **notes:** Actor class **B** (failed to detect/prevent) and **C** (ill-prepared / inadequate investigations). **Not D.** Commissioner Willie L. Jett II, letter June 7, 2024: “MDE disputes the OLA’s characterization regarding the adequacy of MDE’s oversight – MDE’s oversight of these programs met applicable standards and MDE made effective referrals to law enforcement.” “The responsibility for this flagrant fraud lies with the indicted and convicted fraudsters.” (S27)

**Exact quotes (full report / summary):**

1. Transmittal letter (Legislative Auditor Judy Randall): “However, we found MDE’s oversight of Feeding Our Future to be inadequate. In fact, we believe MDE’s actions and inactions created opportunities for fraud.” (S26)
2. Same letter: MDE “Failed to act on warning signs known to the department prior to the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic and prior to the start of the alleged fraud.” (S26)
3. Summary headline: “MDE’s inadequate oversight of Feeding Our Future created opportunities for fraud.” (S27 / S26 Summary p. S-1)
4. “MDE’s only administrative review of Feeding Our Future’s CACFP operations—conducted in 2018—resulted in serious findings that required follow-up, but MDE never conducted a follow-up review. (p. 37)” (S27)
5. “Between June 2018 and December 2021, MDE received at least 30 complaints involving Feeding Our Future or its sites.” (S27 / Chapter 4 opening, p. 49)
6. “And, MDE inappropriately asked Feeding Our Future to investigate complaints about itself. (pp. 49, 51, 55)” (S27)
7. Chapter 4, p. 49: “Most importantly, MDE had reason to doubt Feeding Our Future’s trustworthiness as a sponsor, yet MDE continued to ask Feeding Our Future to resolve complaints about itself.”
8. “MDE did not investigate some complaints about Feeding Our Future, despite their frequency or seriousness. When MDE decided to investigate complaints about Feeding Our Future, the investigations were inadequate. (pp. 53, 57)” (S27)
9. Chapter 4, p. 50: “More broadly, despite repeated warnings by the U.S. Department of Agriculture’s (USDA’s) Office of Inspector General of the risks of fraud, waste, and abuse in federal nutrition programs, MDE was not prepared to investigate these issues in CACFP or SFSP.”
10. Chapter 4, p. 50: MDE “shared its concerns about Feeding Our Future with USDA’s Office of Inspector General in October or November 2020.” Informing USDA “did not absolve MDE of its legal responsibility to (1) ‘promptly investigate’ … and (2) ‘take appropriate action to correct any irregularities.’”

# NINE-BILLION.md — Provenance of the “$9 billion” Medicaid fraud estimate

**Classification of the figure: `fraud_estimate`.**  
**Do not treat $9 billion as stolen, adjudicated loss, or a CMS improper-payment measurement.**

Compiled 26 August 2026. Primary sources first; journalism used only as a lead to the speaker, date, and methodology.

---

## Bottom line (what can be said from primary sources)

| Element | What the record shows |
|---|---|
| **Who** | First Assistant U.S. Attorney **Joseph H. Thompson**, speaking at a **18 December 2025** Minneapolis news conference for the U.S. Attorney’s Office, District of Minnesota (U.S. Attorney Daniel N. Rosen was out of the country). |
| **When** | 18 December 2025, same day as USAO PR charging six additional defendants and unsealing an ICS search warrant. |
| **What he said (as reported; not in the official PR)** | CMS pulled claims since 2018 for **14 Minnesota programs** the State had flagged as high-risk. Those programs had **about $18 billion** in Medicaid billing/cost since 2018 (more than $3.5 billion in 2024 alone, per multiple contemporaneous news accounts of the same conference). A “significant portion” / “half or more” of that $18 billion “could be” fraudulent. Half of $18 billion is the arithmetic origin of **~$9 billion**. Thompson caveated: “But we’ll see.” |
| **Methodology** | **Not a completed forensic audit.** Method described: (1) identify 14 high-risk waiver/state-plan services (State + CMS list); (2) pull CMS claims/cost totals since 2018 = **~$18 billion spending in those services**; (3) apply a prosecutor’s **qualitative** judgment from casework and “red flags” that “half or more” of that spending is fraudulent. **Numerator is not proven loss. Denominator is program spending, not stolen money.** |
| **What it is not** | Not in the official 18 Dec 2025 justice.gov press release. Not a CMS PERM rate. Not a GAO estimate. Not a jury finding. Not an OLA finding. Not DHS’s estimate (DHS IG called the speculation “shocking”; Medicaid Director John Connolly said evidence in hand was “tens of millions,” excluding Feeding Our Future). |
| **Proposition type** | Speaks to **A** (private fraud) at industrial scale, as a **hypothesis**. Does not prove **D**. Mixing the $18 billion spending base with “half or more” fraud is a **B/C** policy argument only if one assumes the State should have stopped the payments — that is a separate inference. |

## 1. The official USAO press release that does *not* contain $9 billion

- **Title:** Six Additional Defendants Charged, One Defendant Pleads Guilty in Ongoing Fraud Schemes
- **Publisher:** U.S. Attorney’s Office, District of Minnesota
- **Date:** 18 December 2025 (updated same day)
- **URL:** https://www.justice.gov/usao-mn/pr/six-additional-defendants-charged-one-defendant-pleads-guilty-ongoing-fraud-schemes
- **Evidence class:** `ENFORCEMENT_CHARGING_OR_OFFICIAL_PR`
- **What it actually says about dollars (primary):**
  - Case-level alleged proceeds: Star Autism **more than $6 million** obtained; HSS schemes of about **$3.5 million** claimed, **$750,000** claimed, **$1.4 million** claimed / **$1.3 million** received; ICS provider Ultimate Home Health **over $1.1 million** billed June 2024–August 2025.
  - **Program-level spending (not labeled as fraud):** HSS predicted **~$2.6 million/year**, then paid **>$21M (2021), $42M (2022), $74M (2023), $104M (2024), $61M in first half of 2025**. ICS: **$4.6 million (2021) → more than $170 million (2024)**; **more than $400 million** since 2021.
- **AUSA Thompson is listed as a prosecutor on the cases; he is not quoted in the PR as stating a statewide $9 billion fraud total.**
- **Classification:** Primary for **A** at the *case and program-growth* level. **Not** a source for $9 billion.

## 2. The $9 billion statement: speaker, date, methodology (journalism as lead)

No justice.gov transcript of the 18 December 2025 news conference was located on 26 August 2026 (`GAPS.md`). Multiple independent news organizations quoted the same speaker the same day. Treat the following as `JOURNALISM_LEAD` pointing to an oral official statement, **not** as a substitute for a transcript.

| Outlet | URL | Attribution (as published) |
|---|---|---|
| Associated Press | https://apnews.com/article/minnesota-fraud-charges-fbad68312012dc02a4060852474f72ee | “Half or more of the roughly $18 billion in federal funds that supported 14 Minnesota-run programs since 2018 may have been stolen, a federal prosecutor said Thursday.” Speaker: First Assistant U.S. Attorney Joe Thompson. |
| CBS Minnesota | https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/billions-paid-out-by-medicaid-in-minnesota-may-be-fraudulent-us-attorney/ | Thompson: 14 high-risk Medicaid services cost **$18 billion since 2018**; “When I say significant amount, I’m talking on the order of half or more. But we’ll see. When I look at the claims data and the providers, I see more red flags than I see legitimate providers.” |
| KSTP | https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/half-or-more-of-18-billion-billed-through-state-programs-tied-to-fraud/ | “CMS pulled claims for 14 programs identified by Minnesota officials as being particularly vulnerable to fraud and found $18 billion in Medicaid billing since 2018.” Thompson estimated “half or more” was received through fraudulent means; “prosecutors are still working to find the exact number.” |
| Minnesota Reformer | https://minnesotareformer.com/2025/12/18/u-s-attorney-fraud-likely-exceeds-9-billion-in-minnesota-run-medicaid-services/ | Same $18B / “half or more” construction; quotes DHS IG James Clark calling the speculation “shocking.” |
| Fox News | https://www.foxnews.com/politics/magnitude-cannot-overstated-feds-say-minnesota-fraud-may-more-than-9b | Quotes Thompson: “The magnitude of fraud in Minnesota cannot be overstated… staggering industrial-scale fraud.” On how much of $18B is fraud: “When I say significant, I’m talking in the order of half or more. But we’ll see.” |

**Evidence class for the $9B figure as used in public debate:** `fraud_estimate`, sourced to an **oral statement by a federal prosecutor**, reconstructed from contemporaneous news accounts pending an official transcript.

**Methodology, restated without rounding up:**

1. **Denominator = spending in 14 high-risk services, 2018–2025**, attributed to a CMS claims pull (Thompson / KSTP / AP). This is **program outlays**, including legitimate care.
2. **Numerator = Thompson’s unquantified judgment** that a “significant portion,” “on the order of half or more,” is fraudulent, based on investigations and “red flags,” not on a completed claims-level audit of $18 billion.
3. **$9 billion = 0.5 × $18 billion**, an arithmetic implication of “half,” not a separately measured loss.

Later journalism (KARE 11 data-practices request, 2026) reported the same 14-program spending base had grown to **$20.83 billion** since 2018. That updates the **denominator**, not proven fraud. https://www.kare11.com/article/news/investigations/fraud/minnesota-spent-20-billion-on-medicaid-programs-at-high-risk-for-fraud-since-2018/89-0e8c18d7-ab84-48f9-aa4d-fce3c7ebed63 — `JOURNALISM_LEAD` / data-practices reporting, not an OLA audit.

## 3. Contemporaneous official pushback (primary)

### 3a. DHS Inspector General James Clark (18–19 December 2025)

Quoted in CBS and Reformer the same day, and in the Duluth News Tribune / Forum 19 December 2025 piece:

- Called Thompson’s speculation that at least half of $18 billion is fraudulent **“shocking.”**
- Asked USAO to share evidence so DHS can suspend payments.
- 19 Dec: “I wouldn’t say it’s wrong… What I’m saying is I haven’t seen any evidence or information to suggest that there’s $9 billion worth of Medicaid [fraud].”

Duluth News Tribune: https://www.duluthnewstribune.com/news/minnesota/gov-tim-walz-dhs-say-they-dont-have-evidence-to-suggest-fraud-could-reach-9-billion  
**Class:** news account of official statements. Seek DHS written statement (`GAPS.md`).

### 3b. Medicaid Director John Connolly (19 December 2025)

Same Forum piece: evidence in hand indicates **“tens of millions of dollars”** in Medicaid fraud, **not including** MDE Feeding Our Future (~$250 million alleged in federal charging). “We don’t have evidence in hand to suggest that we have $9 billion in fraud… these benefits over the last seven years.”

**Class:** official oral statement via journalism. **Proposition:** State’s then-current proven/known fraud stock was orders of magnitude below $9B.

### 3c. CMS PERM (inconsistent with treating $9B as an improper-payment measurement)

CMS letter 20 January 2026: Minnesota RY 2025 overall Medicaid improper-payment rate **2.16%** (payments July 2023–June 2024). https://mn.gov/dhs/assets/2025-medicaid-error-notification_tcm1053-720555.pdf

PERM is a **sample-based improper-payment** measure (documentation, eligibility, processing). CMS’s own letter says improper payments “do not necessarily represent expenses that should not have occurred.” **A 2.16% PERM rate cannot be multiplied by seven years of high-risk-service spending to get $9 billion, and PERM is not designed to detect industrial-scale fabricated-service fraud if documentation is facially complete.**

### 3d. Charged / intended-loss figures from later DOJ primary sources (scale check)

- 2026 Minnesota Health Care Fraud Takedown: **over $90 million intended loss** across 15 defendants. https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/minnesota-health-care-fraud-takedown-results-charges-against-15-defendants-over-90m-fraud
- Feeding Our Future (separate nutrition programs, not Medicaid): federal charging has used **more than $250 million** alleged stolen (OLA and House materials restating USAO). That is **A** in CACFP/SFSP, **not** part of the $18B Medicaid high-risk-services denominator unless someone mixes programs — a common error.

**None of these primary charging totals reach $9 billion.**

## 5. What the 14 programs are (spending base, not fraud proof)

The 30 January 2026 DHS CAP and contemporaneous reporting identify a DHS risk-assessment list of **13 or 14 high-risk services** (counts vary as HSS was terminated). Named in primary DOJ/DHS documents:

- Early Intensive Developmental and Behavioral Intervention (**EIDBI** / autism)
- Housing Stabilization Services (**HSS**) — terminated end of October 2025 with CMS approval (CAP)
- Integrated Community Supports (**ICS**)
- Personal Care Assistance / Community First Services and Supports (**PCA / CFSS**) — majority of the $20B journalism total per KARE 11
- Other HCBS waiver and related services subjected to the Oct/Dec 2025 payment pause and Optum prepayment review (CAP)

**Significant growth is not in itself fraud.** The CAP and DHS statements note legislative rate increases (e.g., night supervision, ICS) as partial non-fraud explanations. Thompson’s methodology treats growth + red flags as a reason to *estimate* a high fraud fraction; that is an investigative hypothesis.

## 6. Recommended citation language for this corpus

**Acceptable:**  
“On 18 December 2025, First Assistant U.S. Attorney Joseph Thompson stated that CMS data showed about $18 billion in claims since 2018 in 14 Minnesota high-risk Medicaid services, and that he believed a significant portion — ‘on the order of half or more’ — could be fraudulent. That statement is a prosecutor’s estimate, not an audited loss. The official USAO press release the same day did not include a $9 billion figure and recited case-level alleged losses in the millions plus program-level spending growth.”

**Not acceptable:**  
“Minnesota lost $9 billion to Medicaid fraud.”  
“CMS found $9 billion in fraud.”  
“OLA confirmed $9 billion.”

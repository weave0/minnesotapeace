## Federal criminal and civil statutes

### 18 U.S.C. § 1343 — Fraud by wire, radio, or television

- **Citation:** 18 U.S.C. § 1343
- **Official URL (OLRC, current prelim):** https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A18+section%3A1343+edition%3Aprelim%29
- **GovInfo HTML (USCODE-2024 Title 18 ch. 63):** https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap63.htm
- **Current-text note:** OLRC page states text in effect as of March 27, 2026 (page retrieved 26 Aug 2026).
- **Core text:** Whoever, having devised or intending to devise any scheme or artifice to defraud, or for obtaining money or property by means of false or fraudulent pretenses, representations, or promises, transmits or causes to be transmitted by means of wire, radio, or television communication in interstate or foreign commerce any writings, signs, signals, pictures, or sounds for the purpose of executing such scheme, shall be fined under this title or imprisoned not more than 20 years, or both (30 years / $1,000,000 if disaster-related or affecting a financial institution).
- **Relevance to MN program fraud:** Principal charging statute in Feeding Our Future, EIDBI/autism, Housing Stabilization Services, and related USAO cases that used interstate wires (claims systems, banks, email) to obtain Medicaid or nutrition reimbursements.

### 18 U.S.C. § 1347 — Health care fraud

- **Citation:** 18 U.S.C. § 1347
- **Official URL:** https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section1347
- **Current-text note:** OLRC page states text in effect as of August 12, 2026.
- **Core text:** Knowingly and willfully executing a scheme to defraud a health care benefit program, or to obtain money or property of such a program by false pretenses, in connection with delivery of or payment for health care items or services. Up to 10 years (20 if serious bodily injury; life if death). Subsection (b): no actual knowledge of the section or specific intent to violate *this section* is required.
- **Relevance:** Directly covers false Medicaid/Medical Assistance billing (EIDBI, PCA/CFSS, ICS, HSS, waiver services). Parallel to § 1343 when the benefit program is Medicaid.

### 18 U.S.C. § 1349 — Attempt and conspiracy (fraud chapter)

- **Citation:** 18 U.S.C. § 1349
- **Official URL:** https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A18+section%3A1349+edition%3Aprelim%29
- **Current-text note:** Text in effect as of August 25, 2026.
- **Core text:** Any person who attempts or conspires to commit any offense under chapter 63 is subject to the same penalties as the object offense.
- **Relevance:** Charged alongside §§ 1343/1347 in multi-defendant Minnesota schemes (including *Kousisis*, which construed §§ 1343 and 1349 together).

### 18 U.S.C. § 666 — Theft or bribery concerning programs receiving Federal funds

- **Citation:** 18 U.S.C. § 666
- **Official URL:** https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A18+section%3A666+edition%3Aprelim%29
- **Current-text note:** Text in effect as of August 9, 2026.
- **Core text:** Agent of an organization, government, or agency that receives >$10,000 in federal benefits in a one-year period who embezzles, steals, obtains by fraud, or intentionally misapplies property of $5,000 or more; or who corruptly solicits/accepts or gives anything of value intending to be influenced or rewarded in a $5,000+ transaction. Up to 10 years.
- **Relevance:** Covers theft/bribery from state agencies and nonprofit sponsors administering federal nutrition, Medicaid, CCAP, or grant dollars. Constrained by *Sabri* (constitutionality) and *Fischer* (what counts as “benefits”).

### 18 U.S.C. § 1956 — Laundering of monetary instruments

- **Citation:** 18 U.S.C. § 1956
- **Official URL:** https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section1956&num=0&edition=prelim
- **GovInfo ch. 95 locator:** https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap95.htm
- **Relevance:** Predicate specified unlawful activity includes mail/wire/health-care fraud. Used when Minnesota defendants moved proceeds (real estate, overseas wires, vehicles, crypto) to conceal or promote the scheme.

### 18 U.S.C. § 1957 — Engaging in monetary transactions in property derived from specified unlawful activity

- **Citation:** 18 U.S.C. § 1957
- **Official URL:** https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section1957&num=0&edition=prelim
- **Relevance:** Criminalizes monetary transactions >$10,000 in criminally derived property; unlike § 1956, does not require proof of a concealment design. Fits large downstream purchases from Medicaid/nutrition proceeds.

### 18 U.S.C. § 1512 — Tampering with a witness, victim, or an informant

- **Citation:** 18 U.S.C. § 1512
- **Official URL:** https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section1512&num=0&edition=prelim
- **Relevance:** Obstruction statute for killing, threatening, corruptly persuading, or misleading conduct toward witnesses/informants, and (after *Fischer v. United States* on a different obstruction subsection, § 1512(c)(2), in a later 2024 case of the same name—**do not conflate**; the 2000 *Fischer* in this corpus is the § 666 benefits case). Relevant if a Minnesota investigation involves threats, document destruction after a subpoena, or witness tampering. USAO 18 Dec 2025 release describes a defendant who fled after a grand-jury subpoena; that is charging narrative, not a § 1512 conviction.

### 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b(b) — Federal anti-kickback statute (AKS)

- **Citation:** 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b, especially subsection (b); also (g) (FCA falsity) and (h) (no specific-intent-to-violate-this-section requirement)
- **Official URL:** https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A42+section%3A1320a-7b+edition%3Aprelim%29
- **Current-text note:** Text in effect as of August 25, 2026.
- **Core (b):** Knowingly and willfully soliciting/receiving or offering/paying remuneration to induce or in return for referrals, or for purchasing/ordering items or services payable by a Federal health care program. Felony: up to 10 years / $100,000 (current penalties after 2018 amendments). Safe harbors in (b)(3).
- **(g):** A claim that includes items or services **resulting from** a violation of this section is a false or fraudulent claim for FCA purposes. That “resulting from” phrase is what *Cairns* (8th Cir.) reads as but-for causation.
- **Relevance:** Parent-kickback recruitment in EIDBI/autism cases, and any Minnesota Medicaid referral-for-pay scheme. Minnesota enacted a parallel crime at Minn. Stat. § 609.542.

### 31 U.S.C. §§ 3729–3733 — False Claims Act

- **Citation:** 31 U.S.C. §§ 3729–3733
- **Official URLs:**
  - § 3729: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section3729&num=0&edition=prelim
  - § 3730: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section3730&num=0&edition=prelim
  - § 3731: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section3731&num=0&edition=prelim
  - § 3732: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section3732&num=0&edition=prelim
  - § 3733: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section3733&num=0&edition=prelim
- **Core:** Civil liability for knowingly presenting a false or fraudulent claim (§ 3729(a)); “knowingly” = actual knowledge, deliberate ignorance, or reckless disregard, **and no proof of specific intent to defraud is required** (§ 3729(b)(1)). Qui tam (§ 3730), preponderance standard for all essential elements (§ 3731(d)), CID authority (§ 3733).
- **Relevance:** Civil recovery parallel to criminal Medicaid fraud; *Escobar* (implied certification / materiality) and *SuperValu* (subjective scienter) control. Minnesota has a state analog in chapter 15C.

## Minnesota statutes

### Minn. Stat. ch. 15C — False Claims Against the State

- **Chapter table:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/15C
- **§ 15C.02 Liability:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/15C.02
- **Current-text note:** 2025 Minnesota Statutes page (retrieved 26 Aug 2026).
- **Core (§ 15C.02):** Civil penalty in the amounts set forth in 31 U.S.C. § 3729 (as inflation-adjusted) **plus treble damages** for seven listed acts (false claims, false records, conspiracy, reverse false claims, etc.). Reduced damages (not less than double) for timely self-disclosure. **“A person is not liable under this section for mere negligence, inadvertence, or mistake.”** (§ 15C.02(d)).
- **Relevance:** State FCA for Medical Assistance and other state payments; qui tam path for MN-only or joint state/federal recoveries. Kickback-tainted claims are expressly false under § 609.542, subd. 6, for 15C.02 purposes.

### Minn. Stat. § 609.466 — Medical Assistance fraud (repealed 2026)

- **URL (still displays historical text with repeal banner):** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/609.466
- **Repeal:** 2026 Minn. Laws ch. 127, art. 7, § 7 — https://www.revisor.mn.gov/laws/2026/0/Session+Law/Chapter/127/
- **Historical core:** Presenting a claim, cost report, or rate application relating to MA funds that is false in whole or in part, with intent to defraud, is an attempt to commit theft of public funds.
- **Relevance:** Pre-MAP Act charging statute. Replaced by broader MAP Act Medical Assistance fraud provisions (new coding in ch. 609 per ch. 127). Do not cite 609.466 as current law after the 1 August 2026 MAP Act effective date without checking the session-law effective-date section.

### Minn. Stat. § 609.542 — Illegal remunerations (state AKS analog)

- **URL:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/609.542
- **Enacted:** 2025 c 38 art 5 s 32
- **Core:** Criminalizes soliciting/receiving or offering/providing anything of value in return for referrals, purchasing/ordering, or applying for items payable by a federal health care program, ch. 254B behavioral health, or ch. 142E (child care). Incorporates federal AKS exceptions. Penalties scale with value (up to 20 years / $100,000 if >$35,000). **Subd. 6:** a claim including items or services resulting from a violation is a false claim under § 15C.02.
- **Relevance:** Direct state charging tool for parent-kickbacks and referral-for-pay in Medicaid, SUD grants, and CCAP. Aligns Minnesota with 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b(b).

### Minn. Stat. § 256B.064 — Sanctions; monetary recovery (payment withhold)

- **URL:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256B.064
- **Current-text note:** 2025 Statutes text displayed; **heavily amended by 2026 c 121 art 3** (new subds. 2a–2g, 6, 8). Use session law for post-codification language: https://www.revisor.mn.gov/laws/ (ch. 121, 2026).
- **Core (displayed text):** Commissioner may sanction for fraud, theft, abuse, false/duplicate claims, false statements, Medicare exclusion, records refusal, unpaid overpayments. **Subd. 2(b):** shall withhold or reduce payments without advance notice if (1) conviction for listed conduct or (2) **credible allegation of fraud** for which an investigation is pending — incorporating 42 C.F.R. § 455.23. Notice within five days unless law enforcement asks to delay. Forfeiture of withheld payments upon conviction.
- **Subd. 1a(c):** sanctions for illegal remuneration under § 609.542 or 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b(b), **no conviction required**.
- **Relevance:** The state-law engine for stopping Medicaid payments. Central to the oversight debate (did DHS have authority to stop Feeding Our Future / HSS / ICS payments without a court order?). CMS and House Oversight allege under-use; DHS CAP recites aggressive 2025 withholds.

### Minn. Stat. § 256B.04 — Duties of state agency (provider enrollment / investigation)

- **URL:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256B.04
- **Key subdivisions:**
  - **Subd. 10** — investigate suspected MA fraud, theft, abuse, false claims; inform the attorney general in writing if civil or criminal proceedings appear warranted.
  - **Subd. 15** — utilization review (pre- and post-payment).
  - **Subd. 21** — provider enrollment and revalidation (42 C.F.R. Part 455 Subpart E); high-risk screening, fingerprinting, unannounced site visits, 90-day payment withhold on initial enrollment for CMS/state-designated high-risk categories; compliance-officer mandate for certain licensed providers; surety bonds for DMEPOS.
- **2026 amendments:** subd. 21 amended by 2026 c 121 art 3 s 15 and 2026 c 95 art 4 s 12 (not fully merged on the Revisor display as of retrieval).
- **Relevance:** Legal basis for Minnesota Revalidate 2026 / off-cycle high-risk revalidation described in the 30 Jan 2026 CAP.

### Minn. Stat. § 245.095 — Limits on receiving public funds (cross-program exclusion and withhold)

- **URL:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/245.095
- **Core:** If a provider is excluded from any DHS-administered program, the commissioner shall bar them from other DHS programs and may bar associated entities/individuals. Subd. 5: may **withhold payments** on a credible allegation of fraud pending investigation, including allegations from other state or federal programs. Subd. 6: data-sharing for fraud investigations.
- **Relevance:** Cross-program “follow the actor” tool (nutrition → child care → Medicaid). Cited in the 30 Jan 2026 CAP as originating in a 2019 DHS proposal.

### Minn. Stat. § 256R.04, subd. 5 — Nursing-facility kickbacks

- **URL:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256R.04
- **Relevance:** Facility-specific kickback bar with treble civil damages; narrower than § 609.542 but still a MA integrity provision.

### MAP Act / 2026 session laws

- **Medical Assistance Protection Act** — enacted as part of **2026 Minn. Laws ch. 127**, signed 26 May 2026, effective (per AG) 1 August 2026.
- **Session law URL:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/laws/2026/0/Session+Law/Chapter/127/
- **What the session law does (from Revisor banners + AG primary releases, not journalism):**
  - Repeals § 609.466 and proposes new law in ch. 609 (broader MA fraud, including false enrollment, record falsification, record destruction during investigation).
  - Adds MA fraud to racketeering (ch. 609).
  - New penalty tiers over $100,000 and over $1,000,000 (AG 3 Aug 2026: >$1,000,000 set at Sentencing Guidelines Severity Level 8 after AG intervention).
  - MFCU staffing 32 → 50; criminal-investigation subpoena authority for financial records.
  - Amends § 256B.04, subd. 27 (ch. 127 art 6 s 3).
- **Relevance:** Current (as of 1 Aug 2026) state criminal architecture for Medical Assistance fraud. For charging after that date, cite ch. 127 / newly coded 609 sections, not repealed 609.466.

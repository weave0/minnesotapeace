# Feeding Our Future — warnings, authority, response, and what the record actually says

**As of:** 29 August 2026  
**Machine-readable timeline:** `research/oversight/fof-warning-response-timeline.json`

This report is not an argument that every warning was correct, every government response was ineffective, or any public official joined the criminal scheme. It is a source-bound chronology answering a narrower question: **what warnings existed, what authority/actions followed, and what later auditors/courts/prosecutors established?**

## Before the alleged pandemic-era scheme

### June 2018 onward — complaints begin

The Office of the Legislative Auditor (OLA) found that the Minnesota Department of Education (MDE) received **at least 30 complaints** involving Feeding Our Future or its sites between June 2018 and December 2021.

OLA also found MDE's complaint practices were often inappropriate or of limited usefulness. In particular, for recruitment complaints beginning in 2018, OLA found MDE repeatedly assigned initial fact-finding and dispute resolution to Feeding Our Future and the complainant even though MDE had reason to doubt the sponsor's trustworthiness.

Primary audit source: `research/sources/src-mn-ola-2024-fof-oversight.json`  
Bound locators: report pp.49 and 55; PDF indexes 56 and 62.

**Qualification:** a complaint is an allegation. The important fact here is the warning/oversight record, not an assumption that all 30 complaints were true.

### December 2018 — administrative review produces 22 findings

OLA found MDE's only CACFP administrative review of Feeding Our Future, conducted in December 2018, produced **22 findings across 11 of 13 review areas**. The findings included issues in areas capable of increasing fraud risk, and OLA concluded follow-up was warranted. MDE never conducted the follow-up administrative review it had told Feeding Our Future would occur.

OLA says MDE later explained a scheduled 2020 follow-up did not happen because of the pandemic, although a desk review could have been performed. OLA also noted the waivers in effect did not prohibit site visits.

This is a particularly important pre-pandemic control point: it establishes documented oversight problems without requiring any inference about criminal intent.

## 2020 — escalation and incomplete review

MDE began an SFSP administrative review in August 2020 and paused it in October when virtual site visits were not working. OLA questioned the decision not to use other approaches such as desk reviews or socially distanced observations.

On **October 12, 2020**, OLA's timeline records a sponsor complaint alleging fraud in CACFP/SFSP. Separately, MDE officials told OLA that MDE escalated concerns to **USDA's Office of Inspector General in October or November 2020**, primarily concerning Feeding Our Future's growth. OLA says the exact date is unclear.

This matters in both directions:

- MDE was not oblivious to risk; it did make a federal referral.
- OLA concluded that referral did **not** eliminate MDE's own regulatory duty to investigate complaints and correct irregularities.

The corpus still lacks USDA OIG's own Minnesota-specific work product showing what the OIG did after receiving that referral.

## The civil case was about applications — not an order to resume payments

Feeding Our Future sued MDE in November 2020. The Minnesota Judicial Branch states the original lawsuit concerned MDE's handling of applications for new food-distribution sites and race-discrimination claims.

The parties agreed to a December 22, 2020 consent order requiring MDE to handle the federally regulated site applications **reasonably promptly**.

That order should not be rewritten as a reimbursement-payment order.

Primary judicial source: `research/sources/src-mn-judicial-2022-09-23-fof-guthmann-correction.json`.

## 2021 — MDE exercises authority, refers concerns, then defers deficiencies

### January–March — serious deficiencies

MDE issued serious deficiencies in January 2021. It initiated a process to terminate Feeding Our Future's CACFP sponsorship on **March 29**, concluding the first deficiencies were not fully corrected.

On **March 31**, MDE issued a second set of serious deficiencies involving financial viability/management, administrative capabilities, and program accountability. OLA's timeline says MDE also stopped reimbursement payments and stopped issuing site IDs for new Feeding Our Future sites.

### April 1 — USDA regional office says MDE has stop-payment authority, according to MDE

OLA records that MDE and the USDA Midwest Regional Office met about the serious-deficiency and proposed-termination process. According to MDE, USDA supported the decision to stop paying reimbursement claims and indicated MDE had authority to do so.

This is stronger evidence than the popular claim that a judge forced MDE to pay. It is still an **OLA record of MDE's account** of USDA's position; obtaining the contemporaneous USDA record would strengthen it further.

### April–May — FBI referral/investigation

OLA records that MDE provided the FBI information in April 2021 about suspected fraud by Feeding Our Future/sites, including suspected fraudulent documents and inflated meal counts. OLA states the FBI began an investigation in May 2021.

### The court did not order payments resumed

After MDE suspended payments, Feeding Our Future asked Judge John Guthmann to order payment resumption and sanctions. The Minnesota Judicial Branch later issued an explicit public correction:

- Judge Guthmann **never** ordered MDE to resume reimbursements;
- MDE resumed payments **voluntarily**;
- MDE asked the court to treat the nonpayment-sanctions issue as moot after it voluntarily resumed paying;
- MDE told the court Feeding Our Future's serious deficiencies were resolved as of June 4, 2021.

The Judicial Branch says **all** MDE food-reimbursement payments to Feeding Our Future were made voluntarily, without a court order requiring payment.

This resolves the narrow factual dispute about who ordered payment resumption. It does **not** by itself answer why MDE chose to resume payments or prove criminal intent.

### June 4 — serious deficiencies deferred

MDE deferred the second serious deficiencies by June 4. OLA says Feeding Our Future was then no longer under any serious deficiencies and could continue operating.

OLA's later conclusion is consequential: it found MDE deferred all serious deficiencies **without taking reasonable steps to ensure corrective actions were implemented** and without sufficient evidence that the deficiencies had been fully and permanently addressed.

## 2022 — federal case becomes public and 47 defendants are charged

The law-enforcement investigation became public in January 2022. On September 20, DOJ announced the initial charges against **47 defendants**, alleging a roughly **$250 million** scheme. DOJ said Feeding Our Future had opened more than 250 sites and alleged more than $240 million was fraudulently obtained/disbursed.

Primary charging source: `research/sources/src-doj-2022-09-20-fof-47-charged.json`.

The $250 million figure is a charging-scheme figure, **not** a restitution-collected total.

Three days later, the Minnesota Judicial Branch issued its formal correction of public statements claiming Judge Guthmann had forced MDE to resume reimbursements.

## 2024 — OLA's overall judgment

OLA concluded in June 2024 that **MDE's inadequate oversight created opportunities for fraud**. Its findings encompass application/administrative reviews, complaint handling, warning signs and the serious-deficiency process.

MDE Commissioner Willie Jett disputed OLA's characterization of the adequacy of MDE oversight, said responsibility for the fraud lay with the indicted/convicted perpetrators, and pointed to reforms including an Office of Inspector General, General Counsel, fraud-reporting training and financial-review work.

Both belong in the record:

- OLA's findings are `AUDIT_FINDING`.
- MDE's response is `AGENCY_POSITION`.

## What can responsibly be concluded

The evidence supports all of the following at once:

1. **Warnings existed years before the federal charges.** Complaints began in 2018 and MDE's own 2018 administrative review identified significant issues.
2. **MDE did take consequential actions.** It escalated concerns to USDA OIG, issued serious deficiencies, initiated termination, stopped payments, and provided fraud concerns to the FBI.
3. **OLA found those actions and MDE's broader oversight insufficient.** It specifically identified missed follow-up, inadequate complaint investigations and deficient serious-deficiency follow-through.
4. **The court did not force MDE to resume payments.** The Minnesota Judicial Branch says MDE resumed voluntarily.
5. **Nothing in these sources establishes that a named MDE official or statewide elected official knowingly joined the Feeding Our Future criminal conspiracy.** That proposition would require actor-specific evidence of criminal participation, not an inference from administrative failure.

That five-part distinction should be preserved in the public site. Removing any one of those points would make the story less accurate.

- Complete Ramsey County civil docket/orders underlying the 2020–2021 litigation.

Until those are acquired, the site should state what the existing records show and label the remaining questions rather than fill them with political inference.

# Notice-chain interviews — Minnesota program-integrity research

**As of:** 2026-08-29  
**Sources:** House Oversight transcribed interviews of Tony Lourey (Feb. 6), Christopher Schmitter (Feb. 27), and Emily Honer (Jan. 29), published by the Committee in June 2026.  
**Publication state:** research only.

## 1. Evidence-class correction

These interviews were **not sworn**. In each transcript the Committee expressly told the witness that:

- participation was voluntary;
- the witness would not be sworn;
- federal law still required truthful answers to Congress and congressional staff;
- knowingly false statements could lead to criminal prosecution.

The research taxonomy has therefore been corrected. `SWORN_TESTIMONY` now requires an actual oath or affirmation. These records use the new `TRANSCRIBED_INTERVIEW` class.

That distinction matters: an official congressional transcript does not turn an unsworn staff interview into sworn evidence simply because it is formal or recorded.

## 2. 2019 DHS → Governor's chief of staff is now directly supported

Former DHS Commissioner Tony Lourey testified that in **2019** he communicated with Governor's Chief of Staff Christopher Schmitter about program-integrity concerns and the tools/authority DHS needed relating to:

- medication-assisted therapy;
- nonemergency medical transportation (NEMT);
- Child Care Assistance Program (CCAP).

This establishes a 2019 program-integrity communication path from the DHS commissioner to the Governor's chief of staff.

It does **not** establish the next arrow to Governor Walz.

When asked whether Schmitter then passed those concerns to the Governor, Lourey explicitly said he could not speak to conversations he was not involved in. When the interviewer then expressly asked Lourey to **speculate** whether concerns at that level likely reached Walz, Lourey said it was likely.

That is stored as speculation, not personal knowledge.

## 3. Schmitter's recollection partly overlaps and partly differs

Schmitter independently recalled significant 2019 program-integrity issues, especially Tribal MAT, and said that issue was the kind that likely would have moved through the Governor's legal team, to him, and then to the Governor.

But when specifically asked whether Lourey communicated NEMT or CCAP fraud concerns to him, Schmitter said he did not remember those communications.

This is **not** recorded as a contradiction requiring one witness to be wrong. One witness affirmatively recalls the communication; the other repeatedly says he does not remember it years later.

The corpus therefore records:

- Lourey → Schmitter, 2019 program-integrity communication: supported by Lourey's direct recollection;
- Schmitter recollection of Tribal MAT/integrity context: supported independently;
- Schmitter recall of Lourey's NEMT/CCAP communications: absent;
- specific Schmitter → Walz notice for those programs: unresolved.

## 4. Feeding Our Future: general 2020 office awareness, specific April notice unresolved

Schmitter said he believed the Governor's office was generally aware of Feeding Our Future litigation in **2020**.

He did **not** remember being specifically briefed when:

- MDE denied Feeding Our Future site applications in April 2020;
- Feeding Our Future served MDE with a draft complaint/litigation hold at the end of April 2020.

He said he did not know whether anyone else in the Governor's office was specifically briefed on the litigation hold and explained that agencies routinely faced substantial litigation, so not every matter or hold necessarily reached the Governor's office in the same way.

He recalled serious **criminal fraud** allegations involving FOF only around the January 2022 federal raids.

This sharpens the March hearing record. Walz later testified under oath that he believed MDE/agency awareness by May 2020 was correct while distinguishing agency awareness from his own personal notice. The combined evidence supports general agency/office awareness of litigation/concerns in 2020; it does not yet establish a precise April 28, 2020 notice to Walz personally.

## 5. USDA OIG referral: substantially stronger chronology

Emily Honer's interview significantly strengthens the late-2020 federal-referral record.

Honer recalled:

- a formal USDA OIG complaint around **October 2020**;
- Monica Herrera making the formal complaint, with Honer helping prepare it;
- Honer continuing to advocate for USDA OIG assistance from roughly November 2020 through February 2021;
- finding the USDA OIG response insufficient and describing it as absent;
- no recollected substantive USDA OIG response regarding FOF until after the January 2022 FBI raids became public.

OLA independently records MDE officials saying MDE shared FOF concerns with USDA OIG in **October or November 2020**.

The **referral itself** is therefore now strongly supported by two primary/institutional records.

What remains missing is USDA OIG's own contemporaneous correspondence, case opening/declination record, referral disposition, or work product. Honer's recollection is not a substitute for USDA OIG's internal record.

## 6. FBI and USAO escalation

Honer recalled FBI conversations beginning around **March 2021**, recurring approximately every two to four weeks, focused on Feeding Our Future's growth, MDE's exhausted administrative tools, and a need for investigative assistance.

OLA separately records MDE providing the FBI fraud-suspicion information in **April 2021** and says the FBI began an investigation in **May 2021**.

Those records are consistent with a spring-2021 federal investigative escalation, though they describe slightly different milestones.

Honer further recalled the U.S. Attorney's Office becoming involved around **August or September 2021** and said DOJ did **not** instruct MDE to continue funding Feeding Our Future despite outstanding concerns.

## 7. Payment-resumption chain remains narrower than political claims

Schmitter said he could not recall the Governor's office being involved in MDE's voluntary resumption of Feeding Our Future payments in 2021. He repeatedly qualified this by saying he did not remember the specifics of that period.

Separately, the Minnesota Judicial Branch has already established that Judge Guthmann did **not** order MDE to resume reimbursements.

The current record therefore supports:

- payment resumption was not court-ordered;
- Schmitter does not recall Governor's-office involvement in the resumption;
- the exact internal MDE decision chain remains a separate question.

A lack of Schmitter recollection should not be inflated into proof that no executive-office discussion occurred.

## 8. Interview denials vs. retaliation/cover-up allegations

Schmitter denied that he or Walz discouraged fraud reporting, investigations, or criminal referrals and denied intimidation of employees. He also denied that Walz or Ellison directed him to ignore fraud.

Lourey likewise denied that Walz or Ellison directed him to ignore fraud and said he had no reason to believe either knowingly ignored fraud within DHS programs.

These interview records materially counter the stronger cover-up/retaliation allegations offered by Part I hearing witnesses and committee members.

They do **not**, by themselves, independently resolve individual whistleblower retaliation claims. Those require personnel files, complaints, investigations, court records, or other actor-specific evidence.

## 9. What the notice chain now permits us to say

The current primary/official record supports this careful ladder:

1. Public and agency fraud/program-integrity concerns existed before and during 2019.
2. Lourey recalls communicating 2019 program-integrity concerns directly to Schmitter.
3. Lourey cannot say Schmitter briefed Walz; his statement that such a briefing was likely was expressly speculative.
4. Schmitter recalls at least some significant 2019 integrity issues reaching his office and gives Tribal MAT as an example he believes would have reached Walz.
5. Schmitter supports general Governor's-office awareness of FOF litigation in 2020 but does not recall specific April 2020 FOF briefings.
6. Honer + OLA strongly support a late-2020 USDA OIG referral.
7. Honer + OLA support spring-2021 FBI investigative escalation; Honer recalls USAO involvement by Aug./Sept. 2021.
8. Serious criminal allegations became unmistakable publicly with the January 2022 raids.
9. None of these records is a charging instrument alleging that a Minnesota elected/state official joined the fraud schemes.

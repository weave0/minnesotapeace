## 6. Minnesota Medical Assistance Fraud law — primary-source gap closed

The earlier harvest had only an Attorney General description of the 2026 law. That gap is now closed with the enacted text and the Sentencing Guidelines.

### 6.1 Enacted offense — Minn. Stat. § 609.467

Primary source: **2026 Minn. Laws ch. 127, art. 7**, Office of the Revisor of Statutes.  
URL: https://www.revisor.mn.gov/laws/2026/0/Session%2BLaw/Chapter/127/  
Local source record: `research/sources/src-mn-laws-2026-ch127-art7-medical-assistance-fraud.json`

The new statute creates Medical Assistance Fraud and, among other specified conduct, addresses intentional fraudulent schemes/claims, false enrollment information, prohibited ownership/operation, false or altered records, specified PCA/CFSS false claims, and intentional destruction of records after lawful request.

Penalty tiers in the enacted text:

- loss **$100,000 or less**: maximum 10 years / $20,000 fine;
- loss **over $100,000 through $1,000,000**: maximum 20 years / $100,000 fine;
- loss **over $1,000,000**: maximum 30 years / $1,000,000 fine.

The law also contains a restitution provision for certain similar related conduct subject to notice and the prosecution's burden of proof.

**Effective date:** **August 1, 2026**, applying to crimes committed on or after that date. Do not apply the new offense or penalty tiers retroactively to older conduct.

### 6.2 Minnesota Sentencing Guidelines rankings

Primary source: Minnesota Sentencing Guidelines Commission, 2026 adopted modifications / 2026 Guidelines.  
Adopted modifications: https://mn.gov/sentencing-guidelines/assets/2026_Adopted_Modifications_Minn_Sentencing_Guidelines_tcm30-762327.pdf  
Guidelines: https://mn.gov/sentencing-guidelines/assets/2026_Minn_Sentencing_Guidelines_Commentary_tcm30-762325.pdf  
Local source record: `research/sources/src-mn-msgc-2026-medical-assistance-fraud-rankings.json`

Confirmed offense severity rankings:

- Medical Assistance Fraud, $100,000 or less → **Severity Level 2**;
- $100,001–$1,000,000 → **Severity Level 6**;
- over $1,000,000 → **Severity Level 8**.

Severity level is not itself an individual sentence. Criminal-history score, grid rules, departures, effective date and other sentencing law still matter.

The Attorney General's Aug. 3 release is retained as an agency explanation, but public legal exposition should cite the enacted law and Guidelines first.

## 10. Publication rules preserved

- A sentence is an adjudicated court outcome; a government release can establish that event, but the judgment remains the preferred source for exact financial obligations.
- A guilty plea resolves guilt as to the pleaded offense; it does not make every amount in every earlier charging allegation a judgment-certified `proven_loss` figure.
- A restitution order is not a recovered-cash total.
- A program-integrity case is not necessarily a criminal case.
- A flagged application is not necessarily completed fraud.
- Statutory maximum penalties are not presumptive sentences.
- Effective dates matter; new 2026 offenses cannot simply be projected backward onto earlier conduct.
- No political or institutional actor is assigned criminal intent without actor-specific primary evidence.

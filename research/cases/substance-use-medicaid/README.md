# Minnesota Medicaid substance-use treatment — case and control family

**As of:** 2026-08-29  
**Scope:** Medicaid-funded substance-use-disorder treatment, related kickback/false-claim enforcement, and the Section 1115 SUD System Reform demonstration's current program-integrity controls.

This family is distinct from Feeding Our Future, HSS, and the 14-service high-risk matrix. Some providers or beneficiaries can appear in adjacent systems, but relationships should be joined only where a source supports them.

## What the public program is for

Minnesota's Section 1115 Substance Use Disorder System Reform demonstration supports a continuum of Medicaid SUD treatment, including outpatient and intensive outpatient treatment, medication-assisted treatment, residential/inpatient treatment, and medically supervised withdrawal management. CMS approved the current demonstration period through March 31, 2031.

Primary program/control source:

- `src-cms-2026-08-04-mn-sud-program-integrity-plan`

CMS approved Minnesota's SUD Program Integrity Plan on August 4, 2026. The plan is broader than the SUD demonstration itself: it describes statewide Medicaid controls including provider screening, pre/post-payment review, utilization review, provider revalidation, data analytics, law-enforcement referrals, and remediation of identified vulnerabilities.

## Enforcement cluster 1 — NUWAY Alliance

Primary sources:

- USAO-MN, June 26, 2025: `src-usao-mn-2025-06-26-nuway-medicaid-settlement`
- Minnesota AG companion release is linked in that source record.

Disposition: `CIVIL_ALLEGED_OR_RESOLVED`.

NUWAY Alliance agreed to pay **$18.5 million** to resolve False Claims Act / Anti-Kickback allegations involving intensive outpatient SUD treatment. The United States alleged that NUWAY paid Medicaid patients to induce reimbursable treatment and separately double-billed overlapping service periods. NUWAY also entered a five-year HHS-OIG Corporate Integrity Agreement with independent claims review.

Minnesota's Attorney General states **more than $8 million** of the settlement will go to Minnesota.

### Money rule

- $18.5M = total civil settlement.
- >$8M = Minnesota share nested inside the total.
- Do **not** add the two.
- DOJ expressly states the resolved claims were allegations and there was no determination of liability or wrongdoing.
- The settlement may overlap Minnesota MFCU's FY2025 aggregate recovery statistics; do not sum without component-level reconciliation.

## Controls now relevant to this family

The CMS-approved Aug. 4, 2026 Program Integrity Plan says Minnesota's current Medicaid controls include:

- provider screening/enrollment controls and high-risk revalidation;
- fee-for-service prepayment review using suspicious-claim flags followed by human review;
- post-payment review and risk dashboards;
- prior authorization and utilization controls;
- referrals of suspected provider fraud to the Minnesota Attorney General/MFCU and collaboration with federal agencies;
- program assessments covering compliance gaps, risk mitigation, utilization monitoring, randomized provider evaluations, oversight procedures, and accountability matrices;
- rules-based/descriptive analytics; the approved plan says current capabilities do **not** include automated decision-making or machine-learning models.

The CMS approval letter makes clear that approval of the plan is not a declaration that vulnerabilities have disappeared: CMS retains compliance/enforcement tools if program-integrity requirements are not met.

## Relationship to Minnesota's broader corrective action plan

The federal-hosted Aug. 4 document includes as Attachment A CMS's signed March 19, 2026 CAP approval letter. That primary federal record states Minnesota's Jan. 30 revision addressed the deficiencies CMS identified and that **the CAP is approved**, while requiring continued implementation and milestone reporting.

## Publication rule

Explain the treatment purpose before the fraud mechanism. Civil settlements, guilty pleas, criminal allegations, program spend, and recoveries must remain different evidence/money classes. A provider's misconduct does not imply that SUD treatment itself is illegitimate or that all SUD providers are suspect.

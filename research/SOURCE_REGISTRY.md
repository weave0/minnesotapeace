# Seed Source Registry

This is a starter registry for primary and high-value sources identified in the first research pass. It is intentionally conservative: entries describe what a source supports, not broader conclusions.

## Feeding Our Future / Child Nutrition

- **U.S. Attorney's Office, District of Minnesota — Feeding Our Future prosecutions.** Use DOJ case releases, charging documents, plea/verdict/sentencing materials, and related court filings to track defendants, alleged loss, adjudicated conduct, restitution, forfeiture, obstruction, and juror-bribery cases.
- **Minnesota Office of the Legislative Auditor — Minnesota Department of Education: Oversight of Feeding Our Future (June 2024).** Key source on MDE oversight weaknesses, complaints, site monitoring, applications, corrective actions, and the agency's handling of concerns.
- **Minnesota Judicial Branch correction regarding Feeding Our Future litigation (Sept. 23, 2022).** Important contradiction record: the Judicial Branch stated that Judge John Guthmann did not order MDE to resume reimbursements; MDE resumed them voluntarily.
- **USDA / USDA OIG materials on federal child nutrition program administration and fraud controls.** Relevant to federal program rules, sponsor/site responsibilities, waivers, and oversight context.

## Medicaid / Housing Stabilization / Autism / HCBS

- **U.S. Attorney's Office, District of Minnesota — Housing Stabilization Services (HSS) fraud prosecutions.** Primary source for charged conduct, defendants, providers, billing amounts, alleged kickbacks/false documentation where charged, and procedural status.
- **U.S. Attorney's Office, District of Minnesota — EIDBI / autism-services fraud prosecutions.** Primary source for charged conduct involving autism-service providers and related Medicaid billing schemes.
- **U.S. Attorney's Office, District of Minnesota — Integrated Community Supports / Individualized Home Supports and other Medicaid fraud cases.** Primary source for charged schemes within HCBS and related services.
- **Minnesota Department of Human Services — program-integrity and high-risk-services materials.** Use for state program definitions, provider enrollment actions, payment withholds, revalidation, service closures, announced enforcement, and official state figures; treat agency claims as agency positions unless independently verified.
- **CMS / Medicaid.gov — Minnesota state-plan amendments and Section 1115 waiver materials.** Use to establish program authorization, federal-state structure, effective dates, benefit requirements, and approved program design.
- **HHS Office of Inspector General — Minnesota Medicaid and child-care audits.** Use for formal audit methodology, improper-payment findings, internal-control findings, and recommendations.
- **Minnesota Attorney General / Medicaid Fraud Control Unit materials.** Use for state-level criminal and civil Medicaid fraud enforcement.

## Child Care / Grants / Other Program Integrity

- **Minnesota child-care program records and HHS/ACF/OIG audits.** Use to distinguish attendance/payment-control findings from proven fraud and to track federal funding requirements.
- **Minnesota Office of the Legislative Auditor — Behavioral Health Administration and grant-management audits.** Use for documented internal-control, monitoring, grant-administration, and oversight findings.
- **Minnesota legislative hearings and committee materials.** Useful for sworn testimony, agency explanations, oversight chronology, and competing political claims; provenance must identify committee, witness, date, and whether statements were sworn.
- **U.S. House/Senate oversight materials regarding Minnesota fraud and Medicaid program integrity.** Use as legislative allegations/testimony unless corroborated by independent primary records.

## Statutes and precedent

### Federal statutory framework

- 18 U.S.C. § 1343 — wire fraud.
- 18 U.S.C. § 1347 — health care fraud.
- 18 U.S.C. § 1349 — attempt and conspiracy relating to fraud offenses.
- 18 U.S.C. § 666 — theft/bribery concerning programs receiving federal funds.
- 18 U.S.C. §§ 1956, 1957 — money laundering / monetary transactions in criminally derived property.
- 18 U.S.C. §§ 1512 et seq. — witness tampering / obstruction provisions when charged.
- 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b(b) — federal Anti-Kickback Statute.
- 31 U.S.C. §§ 3729–3733 — False Claims Act.

### Minnesota framework

- Minnesota False Claims Act, Minn. Stat. ch. 15C.
- Minnesota Medical Assistance fraud and public-program fraud provisions in Minn. Stat. ch. 609 and related human-services statutes; use the Minnesota Revisor's current text and applicable historical versions for conduct-date analysis.
- Minnesota statutes governing provider enrollment, payment suspension/withholding, licensing, kickbacks/remuneration, theft by swindle, identity/document fraud, and conspiracy where relevant to specific cases.

### Precedent to index

- **Universal Health Services, Inc. v. United States ex rel. Escobar, 579 U.S. 176 (2016).** False Claims Act materiality / implied-false-certification framework.
- **United States ex rel. Schutte v. SuperValu Inc., 598 U.S. 739 (2023).** FCA scienter focuses on defendant's knowledge and subjective beliefs, not merely an objectively reasonable interpretation.
- **United States ex rel. Cairns v. D.S. Medical LLC, 42 F.4th 828 (8th Cir. 2022).** Eighth Circuit construction of Anti-Kickback Statute causation in FCA claims based on claims "resulting from" kickbacks.
- **Ciminelli v. United States, 598 U.S. 306 (2023).** Federal wire-fraud statute protects traditional property interests; rejects right-to-control theory.
- **Sabri v. United States, 541 U.S. 600 (2004).** Upholds 18 U.S.C. § 666's application to bribery involving federally funded organizations/governments.
- **Fischer v. United States, 529 U.S. 667 (2000).** Medicare payments may constitute federal benefits for purposes of § 666 depending on the program/entity relationship.
- **Kousisis v. United States (2025).** Index current Supreme Court wire-fraud treatment of fraudulent inducement and economic-property requirements; use the official opinion for precise holding.

## Critical qualification records

- The frequently reported **$9 billion** Minnesota Medicaid fraud figure must be stored as a **fraud estimate / investigative hypothesis**, not proven loss. Any visualization must identify the speaker/source, denominator, methodology, period, and whether the estimate has been audited or adjudicated.
- Fraud committed **against** Minnesota-administered programs is not equivalent to proof that **Minnesota state officials committed fraud**. Claims involving public officials require actor-specific evidence and classification.
- Indictments and complaints are allegations. A defendant is presumed innocent unless and until convicted.
- Audit findings may establish control failures or improper payments without establishing criminal intent or fraud.

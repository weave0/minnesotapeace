Money & recovery · unlike figures stay unlike

There is no honest single “money recovered” number yet.

Minnesota's public record contains cash recoveries, civil settlements, court judgments, restitution orders, money identified for recovery, potential recoupment opportunities and future cost avoidance. They answer different questions. Adding them together would create a number that means nothing.

01 · Read the ledger correctly

Six figures. Six different meanings.

The useful question is not “what is the biggest number?” It is “what does this number legally and financially represent, and has it actually been collected?”

November 2026 · source document review

A forfeiture order is a legal obligation, not money returned.

Newly examined court documents show why a headline amount can mean very different things depending on its source and procedural stage.

Federal court order · Feb. 27, 2026

$7,837,656.60 forfeiture judgment against Salim Ahmed Said

A signed preliminary forfeiture order in the Feeding Our Future case, ECF 805, enters a money judgment for this amount. The order says the net value of forfeited property is credited toward that judgment. It is not an additional amount to add to seized property, and it is not proof that the government received the money. Said was sentenced on October 28; his signed sentencing judgment still needs independent examination for the final financial terms.

Civil allegation · Oct. 26, 2026

More than $10 million in reimbursements cited in EAHN lawsuit

Minnesota's Attorney General alleges that East African Healthcare Network and its vendors received more than $10 million in federal child-nutrition reimbursements during 2020–2022. The complaint distinguishes periods when Partners in Nutrition submitted claims from periods when EAHN sponsored sites directly. Reimbursements received are not the same as court-proven fraud losses; these are allegations, not a judgment.

The separate Nasib Bakery complaint alleges that the Partners in Nutrition-sponsored Riverside Park site claimed over $2.8 million in meal reimbursements from October 2020 through April 2021. Nothing here increases a verified recovery total

The federal forfeiture amount, the state civil allegations and the earlier restitution judgments may refer to different legal measures and overlapping assets or reimbursements. None of these newly reviewed records independently establishes how much cash was collected.

Court records examined November 1, 2026

When more than one defendant or filing points to the same money.

One legal claim can appear in several judgments, and one property can be named in multiple forfeiture orders. The right accounting keeps the underlying obligation and asset in view rather than adding the entries as though each represented new cash.

Federal forfeiture case identified

The government's complaint says these properties were not seized

The original 19-page verified complaint, filed October 19, 2026 as 0:26-cv-04533, ECF 1, names three Minneapolis properties at 2900 and 2906 Pillsbury Avenue and 211 West 29th Street. Paragraph 3 expressly says the properties had not been seized and that the United States was not requesting seizure authority at that time. Later in the same filing, prosecutors seek eventual forfeiture through the court. No court-ordered sale or cash collection is established.

Prosecutors allege the properties were bought by Marcon LLC in July 2021 for $516,058.50 including expenses, including a $400,000 transfer from Fresh Point LLC to a title company. That is an alleged historic purchase transaction, not money recovered. DOJ's approximately $519,000 property valuation is a different measure. The complaint traces the alleged nutrition reimbursements through Partners in Nutrition sponsorship; that organization is separate from Feeding Our Future.

02 · Settlements are not convictions

Resolved money can still carry legal caveats.

Civil settlements can return substantial funds without a criminal conviction. Their own language determines what was admitted, denied or left unresolved.

NUWAY Alliance agreed to pay $18.5 million to resolve Medicaid kickback and false-claims allegations. The federal release expressly says there was no determination of liability or wrongdoing.

Why the FY2025 MFCU total cannot simply be added to NUWAY

The NUWAY settlement occurred in FY2025 and involved Minnesota MFCU. It may therefore already contribute to the unit's reported >$24 million FY2025 recovery total. Without the component ledger, adding the two figures risks double counting.

03 · The rules

Five distinctions prevent a fake grand total.

These rules are simple enough to use on every new case, audit or press release.

1

Ordered ≠ collected

Restitution or a civil judgment creates an obligation. It does not prove the full amount has been paid.

2

Identified ≠ recovered

An agency can identify an overpayment or recovery opportunity before the amount is final or collected.

3

Potential ≠ proven

A screening or analytics estimate can identify questions that later shrink, disappear or become actual recoveries after review.

4

Avoided ≠ returned

Preventing future expense is valuable but is not the same as receiving money back.

5

Nested ≠ additive

State shares, restitution components, joint-and-several orders and annual aggregates can overlap. Do not count the same obligation twice.
